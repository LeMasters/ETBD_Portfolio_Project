# Guidelines for Setting Up Your First Portfolio

19 Nov 2026 · Garrison LeMasters

A portfolio is an argument about you, made out of your work. These twelve habits make the argument easier to believe.

1. **Show only your best work.** Reviewers judge you by your weakest piece, not your average. Three strong projects beat fifteen mixed ones. If you hesitate over a piece, cut it, and leave unfinished work out entirely.
2. **Pick a memorable, future-proof domain name.** Use your own name (`janedoe.com`), not a school, a year, a project or a joke handle; you'll still want it in ten years. Keep it renewed. See [How to Set Up Your Own Domain Name](own-domain.md).
3. **Keep a change log.** Each time you update the site, write the date and one line on what changed and why. It shows how the work developed, makes reviews quicker, and lets you undo a change you regret.
4. **Be consistent, coherent and accurate. Never exaggerate.** Your name, title, dates and roles should match across your portfolio, résumé and LinkedIn. Check every date, credit and spelling. One claim you can't back up in an interview undoes everything around it.
5. **Be exact about who did what.** Name your role on team projects. Say where you used a tutorial, a template, bought assets or AI tools.
6. **Say what you do in the first five seconds.** Put one clear title under your name: "Level Designer", not "Creative". Range can come further down. In Alexander, this is the Area of expertise field in Profile.
7. **Caption everything.** One or two lines per piece: your role, your tools, the problem, the result. Never let the work "speak for itself".
8. **Show your process on at least two projects.** Sketch to final, playtest note to design change. In Alexander, a case study's Sections are made for this. Take screenshots while you work; you can't recreate them later.
9. **Make it easy to contact you.** Your name on every screen, your email and résumé one click away, and a line saying what you're looking for and when you're available.
10. **Describe every image.** Fill in each image's alt text, for readers who can't see the image and for search engines.
11. **Test on every device you can find.** Phones first: reviewers often browse in bed. Try an old laptop, a friend's phone, a slow connection.
12. **Finish, then stop fiddling.** Publish when it's good, not perfect. Then review it once a semester, cut what has been overtaken, and log the change.
