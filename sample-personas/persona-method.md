# Why these personas exist

Fictional composite students ("real composites") for sample portfolios in the Alexander theme. Three uses:
1. Help Garrison revise the theme and the overall portfolio approach.
2. Show other faculty what he's trying to do.
3. Give ~5 early-adopter students a sense of what they're aiming for.

They are temporary. Retire them once real students' portfolios can replace them.

# Using an existing dossier to fill a portfolio template

Read one student's dossier and the supplied template. Start with §9 for the default audience, headline, and featured work. A different audience in the brief selects a different lever in §7. Sections 1–6 and 8 supply the underlying details. The page map is a menu of available material. Follow the supplied template's actual fields, order, and content limits.

| When the template asks for… | Draw from… |
|---|---|
| A headline or bio | §1 and §8. Give the role, then a sentence the student might actually say. |
| A featured project | §4 and §9. Name the work, credit the student's part, show one decision, and say what happened. For collaborative work, distinguish “we made” from “I made.” |
| A result | Use the observation and its scope. John's five-person pilot prompted a repaint; whether the repaint helped is a separate question. Preserve the distinction between completed and planned work. |
| A tool list | §5's tool names. A Note only if it says something general about the tool: how long, how much, or what kind of work (“C#; three years”, “used on two team projects”, “perspective blockouts only”). Never §5's evidence from one project (what was built with it, a weak spot); that belongs in the project's Sections or an interview. When in doubt, leave the Note empty. |
| A gallery or image | Draft a title and caption from the dossier. Keep asset briefs in production notes. Write final alt text from the actual image once available. |
| A link, download, or empty section | Bind a supplied destination or asset. Omit an optional field that has no content. If the schema requires a value, use its documented empty value or flag the missing asset in the handoff. |

Don't add a “fictional” label (see below). Keep role-swap history, alternative narratives, faculty notes, † marks, verification flags, and production instructions out of public copy. Where an official title is unresolved, use the supported general wording (for example, “I study Games + Simulation and Studio Art at Miami”). Draft fresh prose from the supplied events; carry forward team credits, dates, and limits. A mockup, planned release, or suggested screenshot furnishes no evidence of a live URL, a downloadable file, or a later outcome.

**Check each entry against the field it lands in.** Before copying §9 text into a field, read the field's hint in the worksheet or Pages CMS and ask whether a visitor would read the text as the field intends. Text written for one purpose in the dossier (interview evidence, a production note) can read wrongly in another place on the site. Robert's Figma note, 22 Nov 2026, is the example: §5's “three linked screens; spacing still by hand” appeared on his home page as if it described his Figma skills in general.

# Filling an Alexander copy

Each dossier's §9 holds an **entry plan for Alexander's Pages CMS forms** (written for 0.6.4; form names updated for 0.6.6, when Projects became Selected Work), one row per form in Pages CMS order. Follow it field by field; leave every field it doesn't name empty, so empty sections hide. The mechanics (worksheet copy, placeholder images, uploads, pitfalls) are in `_pagescms-fill-runbook.md`.

- **Area of expertise** is a role line, not a slogan. A headline sentence goes at the start of About.
- **Kind:** one case study per persona, as the flagship; other work as *Project*; small or in-progress work as *Brief*. Delete suggested Section headings that have nothing to say. From 0.6.6 the Kind is shown as a small label before each title.
- **Related work** (Experience; "Related project" before 0.6.6) only where the project was the student's own, not an employer's.
- **Tools:** tool names; Notes only as in the table above. Each §9 Tools row says which tools carry a note; if it doesn't say, leave Notes empty.
- **No fictional accounts.** LinkedIn, GitHub, Résumé and Custom Links stay empty; email uses `example.org`.
- **Images:** none exist yet. Each §9 lists what is needed. Upload placeholder grids (runbook §1) until real mockups are made. Alt text describes the intended image; rewrite it from the actual image when one exists.
- **Appearance:** each persona's plan varies colour scheme and typefaces, so the set shows the range.

**No “fictional” label** (decided by Garrison, 22 Nov 2026). The generic details, the poets' names and the `example.org` emails are enough. Don't add one.

# Requirements

- **Coherent:** reads as one plausible person; details reinforce each other.
- **Aspirational:** a believable Miami undergraduate in the named major and semester. Match portfolio depth to opportunities the student has actually had within the fiction. Not a prodigy: count campus jobs, honours and extracurriculars against the student's year and course load (John lost a campus job and an exhibition for this reason, 22 Nov 2026).
- **Tweakable:** the same data must support several narrative approaches a student could take (see "Narrative levers" below).
- **Details matter.** Concrete names, dates, numbers, tools, and courses, plus honest limitations.
- **No default gender casting.** Across the set, don't map roles to gender by habit (e.g., man = coder, woman = artist). Garrison swapped John and Anne on 22 Nov 2026 for this reason. Check the whole set when adding a persona.

# Inputs Garrison provides

- Name, major/minor, and a career theme (e.g., Anne Southwell: Games major, CSE minor, tools for game developers).
- Sometimes links to real student LinkedIn profiles for inspiration.
  - Borrow only **generic** patterns (kinds of roles, course types, project types). Copy no identifying specifics (names, employers, titles, dates, distinctive projects) from real people, and don't name the people viewed in the persona file. Record what was borrowed and what was deliberately left out in the pass log.

# Process: three passes

1. **Generic pool:** gather a pool appropriate to the student’s year and major. An ordinary paid job and one class exercise can carry an early portfolio. For an older student, consider team projects, work experience, teaching or research where they fit.
2. **Theme:** apply the persona's career theme. Cut irrelevant or filler items, and record what was cut. Reframe what can be kept (e.g., an IT job recast as "removing tedium for others"). Fill gaps with meaningful, theme-specific projects and experiences.
3. **Coherence:** add small realistic extras: an origin story, an off-hours interest that quietly echoes the theme, games played, books read, voice tics, admitted weak spots, project limitations, metrics with a stated source, one memorable line.

# Document structure (mirror the exemplar)

Frontmatter → 0 Provenance/caveats → 1 Identity/header → 2 Education → 3 Experience → 4 Projects → 5 Skills → 6 Activities → 7 Narrative levers → 8 Human details → 9 Page map (Alexander site) → 10 Pass log → 11 Gaps/to-do.

In §5, keep tool names separate from evidence. If the skills table has an evidence column, label it as interview evidence so it isn't mistaken for site copy. In §9's Tools row, say explicitly which tools carry a Note and what it says, or that Notes stay empty.

Tags: **[GAME-PRO] / [ACADEMIC] / [BOTH]** on sections (as in `Alexander/portfolio-raw-material.md`); **†** on invented organizations; **[VERIFY]** on Miami specifics such as course numbers, degree title, and club names.

# Narrative levers

Each persona gets a table of 3–5 honest stories from the same data: for each, the story, the lead project, and what gets demoted or cut. Include a short faculty-facing note that choosing among them is the portfolio's argument. A light humanist/philosophical link is welcome; keep it brief. Levers vary emphasis and market, not the career theme Garrison set (e.g., John's levers keep him a 2D background artist).

# Conventions

- Filenames: `Personna <Full Name>.md` (Garrison's spelling) in `Games_Portfolio_Project/FICTIONAL_PERSONAE/`.
- Names: personas are named after 17th-century English poets (Donne, Southwell, Herrick), so they read as fictional at a glance and form a set.
- Versions: a persona file's version follows its dossier, not its name (the John/Anne swap of 22 Nov 2026 carried each dossier's history to its new name). Record each new version in the dossier's pass log.
- Timeline: by default graduating May of the current academic year, entered four years earlier. A younger persona (e.g., John Donne, a junior) aims the portfolio at an internship; say so in §0.
- Miami course numbers in use: IMS101, 211, 212, 213, 222, 225, 228, 285, 314, 384, 390, 443, 452, 489; CCA195. Mark CSE, ART and other numbers [VERIFY].
- Degree titles confirmed in the Miami Bulletin (22 Nov 2026): **B.S. in Games + Simulation**; **B.A. in Emerging Technology in Business and Design** (the name in a 2025 entrant's bulletin; the department now also presents the major as Interactive Media and Design).
- Engines: **IMS213 Game Development is taught in Unreal Engine 5** (confirmed by Garrison, 22 Nov 2026). Say which engine a course used, and keep it the same across personas who took or taught the same course in the same semester.
- Invented company names must be checked for real-world collisions before publishing.
- Writing style: clear, brief, precise. Lay out options rather than advocating.
- Create personas that contrast with existing ones. Covered: tools programmer (Anne), 2D background artist (John), early-career interaction and web-content work (Robert). Still uncovered: a narrative/game-writing student; a student-founded-studio or producer path. The set is now two men and one woman, and two seniors-or-juniors and one sophomore; weigh both when adding the next.
