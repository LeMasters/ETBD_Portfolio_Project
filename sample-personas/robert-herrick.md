# 4. Projects [BOTH]

## 4.1 *Already Have a Ticket?* · Individual class project, Fall 2026 ★

**Card copy:** “I sketched a mobile arrival guide for a two-screen cinema and tried it with three classmates who had never worked the door.”

**Problem.** The prototype models the cinema where Robert works, unofficially; the cinema has no app. A ticket confirmation tells you what you bought. Here it would leave out the one thing you need on arrival: people who bought online can go straight to the usher. Robert's first sketch buried that instruction behind a button labeled “Admission.” He had carried the box office's vocabulary straight into the design.

**What he made.** A paper sketch followed by a three-screen Figma prototype: the evening's listings, a ticket confirmation, and an arrival guide. He wrote the copy and linked the screens himself. A classmate helped by taking notes while he ran the exercise.

**What changed.** Robert handed each of three classmates the confirmation screen and said, “You've bought your ticket. You're outside and there's a line at the window. Show me what you'd do.” Two went back to the listings. The third found “Admission” and asked whether she was about to buy another ticket. He moved the sentence **“Already have a ticket? Go straight to the usher inside the blue doors”** onto the confirmation itself. Then he drew in the blue doors because that was the detail he'd point to from the booth.

**The draft he crossed out** (added 23 Nov). His shortest draft was “Skip the line.” He crossed it out: on a busy night there are two lines, and somebody with a prepaid ticket still has to wait for the usher. On the site this is the third paragraph of the Iteration section. It is the clearest evidence in the set of a sophomore choosing accuracy over brevity.

**Where it stands.** He has the first sketch, his note-taker's tally sheet, and both versions of the confirmation. The revision still needs a second try with people unfamiliar with the building. The flow ends before payment. His three classmates can tell him where his wording confused them; the cinema has no customer results to report.

**Caption for the comparison image:** “Left: the first confirmation sends you looking for ‘Admission.’ Right: the revised confirmation tells a prepaid visitor which line to skip and which doors to use.”

A half-sheet handbill for an imaginary double feature. Robert printed it on the dorm printer and taped it to his door. From the stairs he could read the film titles but couldn't tell what night to come. He swapped the date and the director credit in the type hierarchy. ## 4.3 *Doors at Six* · Volunteer print piece, Fall 2026 (added 23 Nov)

**Card copy:** “A pocket schedule for a one-night reading with two start times.”

**Problem.** A friend organizing a student reading asked Robert to put the evening on a quarter sheet. Her running order began at 6:30. The person setting out chairs had been told six. Both had the right time. Robert wrote the two things that actually happened: “Doors open 6:00. First reader 6:30.”

**What he made.** Copy, layout and folding of the schedule; the organizer checked the times and reader order. On his first proof the room number ended up inside the fold. He walked from the building entrance with the folded copy in one hand and a stack of programs in the other; at the first stairwell he had to put the stack down. He moved the room number next to the two times on the outside panel. Inside, the organizer could still fit all six readers in running order.

**Where it stands.** The revised copies were used that evening. One guest arrived between doors and the reading and pointed to the two times to ask whether she could go in already. Next time he'd try “Come in from 6:00” and see how it reads to someone arriving alone.

**Why it belongs.** It repeats the case study's habit at a different scale: Robert walks the route a visitor takes, with the visitor's hands full, before he trusts a layout. It also gives faculty a second, smaller project to compare with the case study: a *Project* card with two sections and no section pictures.
